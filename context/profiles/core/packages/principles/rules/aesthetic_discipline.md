# Aesthetic Discipline

When uncertain, restrain. Most bad UI is over-designed, not under-designed: too many colors, too many fonts, too much decoration, too many competing focal points. The disciplined default is to do less, more precisely. When the call is genuinely unclear, choose the quieter option — you can always add; removing after the fact is rarer and harder.

- **Hierarchy over decoration.** A clear order of importance does more for a design than any gradient, shadow, or ornament — solve hierarchy first; reach for decoration last, and only when it encodes something true.
- **One focal point per view.** Decide the single most important element and give it the most contrast; pull contrast *out* of everything else to make room — multiple "primary" things cancel each other out.
- **Spend boldness once.** Let one signature element be the memorable thing and keep everything around it quiet — boldness everywhere is just noise.
- **Reduce before adding.** Before shipping, remove one element — a divider, a color, a weight, an animation; if nothing breaks, it wasn't earning its place.
- **Consistency beats local cleverness.** A clever one-off that breaks the system's patterns weakens the whole; the coherence of the design language is worth more than any single screen's flourish.
- **Match complexity to the vision, then execute fully.** Minimal directions need precision in spacing, type, and detail; maximal directions need complete execution — elegance is doing the chosen thing well, not adding more.
